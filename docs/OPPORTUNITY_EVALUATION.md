## Deeper look at the leading candidates

**A. Sheet-metal cutting quote automation — selected.**
A job shop receives DXF files all day and must answer with a price. The price depends on cut length, pierce count, material weight, nesting yield and machine minute cost, so it is slow and error-prone by hand. Evidence: international products sell at USD 100–900 a month; several Turkish shops built their own instant-price pages; one Turkish competitor (LazerCRM) exists but prices from a CAM report, not from the drawing. Weak points: the market is a niche inside metal fabrication (count unknown), and onboarding needs the shop's own rate tables.

**P. CNC machining instant quoting (3D).** Same buyer logic and higher prices (Oroox from USD 784), but it needs a B-rep CAD kernel and machining feature recognition. That is not realistic for a small team to get right, and a wrong machining price is costly. It is the natural second module once the customer base exists.

**B. Panel/profile cutting optimisation.** The largest potential user count (furniture and window fabricators) and the easiest demo. Rejected on price: free tools and USD 9–20 products set the ceiling, and profile suppliers give software to their dealers.

**C. Load planning.** Clean self-service product with international reach, but established tools at EUR/USD tens per month and little room to differ.

**D. Route optimisation.** Highest measurable savings, but a funded Turkish incumbent with enterprise references, dependence on map and traffic data, and a sales cycle that is not self-service.

**N. Currency-indexed price lists / CPQ.** Scores well only on ease. It is a CRUD product with no technical moat, which the brief excludes; it was not researched further. Its useful part (prices in USD/EUR with a shop-set exchange rate) is folded into the selected product.

## Decision

Build **A**, a quoting platform for sheet-metal cutting job shops: drawing in, defensible price out, plus a branded page where the shop's own customers get that price themselves.

Why it wins on the evidence rather than on the score alone:

1. It is the only candidate where Turkish buyers were seen spending money to solve the problem themselves.
2. The international price band is five to fifty times higher than for the optimisation tools, and the one local competitor takes a different route to the price.
3. The value is checkable by the buyer in minutes: upload a drawing they already quoted and compare.
4. No licence, no government or marketplace API, no hardware, no on-site work.
5. The engine (DXF geometry, contour topology, nesting, time model) is real engineering and carries over to plasma, waterjet, oxy-fuel and router shops, and later to tube and 3D parts.

Main risks, stated plainly: the number of reachable shops is unverified; shops may distrust a computed price until they calibrate it; LazerCRM or a machine builder could add drawing-based pricing. The launch plan therefore starts with paid pilots, not with scale.
